Product Manager · Team of 5 · Università Cattolica · 2026

An AI agent for cross-cultural negotiation

A Claude-based negotiation strategist that turns cultural insight into concrete, testable moves.

PRDAgent designEvaluation
Live agent demoFull markstop grade from the course jury on the agent running live

Problem

Cultural frameworks alone don't make a negotiator. Most "culture tips" stop at stereotypes and never reach a move you can make at the table.

My role

Product Manager for a team of five: owned the PRD, kept the knowledge base, system instructions and evaluation aligned, and drove the iterations.

Built

Followed a production agent workflow: research, a 60+ chunk knowledge base with ~30 sources verified by hand, a PRD with 9 capabilities, and system instructions iterated from v1.0 to v1.2.

Design

Negotiation first (interests, BATNA, ZOPA, leverage), culture second. Cultural patterns are treated as hypotheses to test, never as labels for the person across the table.

Evaluation

Tested on 7 cases across cultural pairs (France–China, France–Japan, US–Indonesia and more). Diagnosed 5 gaps, rewrote the rules, re-ran the case, and documented the weaknesses that remained.

University challenge · Università Cattolica · May 2025

Making public jobs attractive to young people

An employer-branding project for the Italian public administration, “Costruttori di futuro”. Our team won.

User researchInsight → proposalPitch
1stwinning team among the participating groups

Problem

The public sector loses an estimated 25,000 people a year and its average employee is about 52. Young people rarely see it as a career.

Research

The class interviewed 42 public-sector managers on roles and career paths. My team interviewed Luca Manganaro (INPS Milano) and Chiara Bonomo (Comune di Milano).

Proposal

Turned the interview insights into communication actions to make public-sector work more attractive to young people.

Result

Presented in Piacenza to the city's mayor and senior leaders from the Comune di Piacenza, Comune di Monza and Regione Emilia-Romagna. Our project won.

How I work

The same loop on every project, from a client's back office to my own startup.

  1. Listen

    Interviews, surveys, on-site visits. I learn the business before proposing anything.

  2. Map

    Draw the process as it really runs and find where time and errors go.

  3. Build

    The smallest version that proves the point: a demo on real data, a live site, an MVP.

  4. Convince

    Present to the people who decide and get them on board.

About

I love new technology. I believe even more in people.

When I find a new tool, I want to try it on something real right away: a website, an automation, a demo on a client's data. That's how I learned most of what I know.

But the best things I've worked on started with a conversation: a client walking me through a process, a student telling me what she kept missing, a public manager explaining why young people don't apply. I listen first, then build.

I'm at the point of choosing my direction, and I know what I'm looking for: people better than me to learn from, and problems where technology changes someone's day.
